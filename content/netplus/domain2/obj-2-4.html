Structured Cabling Components

ComponentDescription
MDF (Main Distribution Frame)Central point of a structured cabling system. Where ISP connection enters and connects to core switches.
IDF (Intermediate Distribution Frame)Connects floor-level access switches to the MDF via backbone cabling. One per floor or area.
Patch PanelPassive device that organizes and routes cable connections. Front = patch cords to switches; back = runs to wall jacks.
66 BlockOlder punchdown block — used for analog voice (telephone). Not recommended for Cat 5e+ data.
110 BlockModern punchdown block — supports Cat 5e and above for data. Preferred for structured cabling.
Cable TraySupport structure that runs cable horizontally (horizontal cabling) in ceilings or under raised floors.
ConduitProtective tube for cables — EMI shielding, physical protection, and NEC compliance in exposed areas.

Power over Ethernet (PoE) Standards

StandardPower DeliveredUse Case
802.3af (PoE)15.4 W (12.95 W at device)IP phones, basic WAPs, cameras
802.3at (PoE+)30 W (25.5 W at device)Higher-powered WAPs, PTZ cameras, thin clients
802.3bt (PoE++ / 4PPoE)60–90 W (Type 3: 60W, Type 4: 90W)Video conferencing equipment, laptops, small UPS
PoE Budget
Switches have a total PoE budget (e.g., 370W for a 24-port switch). If you add too many high-wattage devices, you exhaust the budget and ports stop powering. Always check the PSE (Power Sourcing Equipment) budget before deploying.

Cable Testing Tools

ToolWhat It TestsOSI Layer
Cable Tester (Continuity)Verifies each wire is connected correctly end-to-end. Detects opens, shorts, reversed pairs, crossed pairs.L1
TDR (Time-Domain Reflectometer)Sends pulse and measures reflection — finds location of breaks or faults in copper cable.L1
OTDR (Optical TDR)Same as TDR but for fiber optic — identifies breaks, splices, connectors and their distance.L1
Optical Power Meter / Light MeterMeasures optical signal power (dBm) — determines if fiber has sufficient signal level.L1
MultimeterMeasures voltage, current, resistance — tests power at outlets, checks for shorts.L1
Toner Probe / Fox & HoundInjects tone on one end; probe detects it to trace cable path through walls or patch panels.L1
Punchdown ToolTerminates wires into a 110 or 66 block or keystone jack.L1 (installation)
Wire CrimperAttaches RJ45 connectors to cable ends.L1 (installation)
Visual Fault Locator (VFL)Injects visible red laser into fiber — visible glow at breaks, bad connectors, or bends.L1

Common Interface Error Types

⚠ Exam Traps
  • Late collisions = duplex mismatch. If you see late collisions, check duplex settings on both sides of the link.
  • OTDR is for fiber. TDR is for copper. Don't mix them up.
  • 66 blocks are for voice/analog. 110 blocks support data (Cat 5e+).
  • 802.3af = 15.4W (older PoE). 802.3at = 30W (PoE+). 802.3bt = 90W (PoE++). Know all three.
  • PoE power budget must be calculated for the entire switch — not just per-port.

ConceptDescriptionKey Detail
Hot AisleAisle where server exhaust ports face — collects hot air from server rearHot air is captured and routed back to CRAC/CRAH cooling units; typically odd-numbered aisles
Cold AisleAisle where server intake ports face — delivers cool air from raised floor or ceilingServers draw cool air from the front; alternates with hot aisles
Hot/Cold Aisle ContainmentPhysical curtains, doors, or panels that separate hot and cold aislesPrevents hot and cold air mixing; significantly improves cooling efficiency and PUE
Port-side IntakeSome network equipment (switches) draws cool air through the port side (front)Front-to-back airflow — intake at ports, exhaust at rear; align with cold-aisle-facing orientation
Port-side ExhaustSome equipment exhausts hot air through the port side (front)Rear-to-front airflow — creates hot air in cold aisle; requires careful placement or separate cooling
Rack Unit (U / RU)Standard measurement of rack height1U = 1.75 inches (44.45 mm); standard rack = 42U; used for capacity planning and asset documentation
Rack DiagramVisual documentation of equipment installed in each rack, by U positionShows device model, U position, cabling; essential for capacity planning and change management
⚠ Datacenter Exam Traps
  • A hot spot inside a rack usually means misaligned airflow — a blanking panel is missing, or equipment with non-standard airflow direction is installed in the wrong orientation.
  • Most servers: intake from front (cold aisle), exhaust from rear (hot aisle). Network equipment may vary — always check manufacturer airflow direction before installing.
  • Containment improves efficiency; without it, hot and cold air mix and cooling systems work harder.
Quick Recall — 2.4
  • MDF = main distribution point | IDF = per-floor access point
  • 66 block = voice | 110 block = data (Cat 5e+)
  • PoE: 802.3af=15.4W | 802.3at=30W | 802.3bt=90W
  • TDR = copper fault location | OTDR = fiber fault location
  • Late collisions = duplex mismatch | Runts = collision/speed issue | Giants = MTU misconfiguration
  • Cold aisle = server intakes face in | Hot aisle = server exhausts face in
  • 1U = 1.75 inches | Standard rack = 42U
  • Hot spot = missing blanking panel or misaligned airflow direction
