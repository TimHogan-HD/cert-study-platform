Switch Ports & VLANs

Configure every port on an access switch, then fix a trunk that stopped carrying one VLAN.

PBQ 1 · Switch configuration

Configure the ports on SW-FL2

SW-FL2 is a new access switch. Configure each port for the device connected to it, using these VLANs:

VLANNameUse
10STAFFEmployee computers and the staff wireless network
20VOICEIP phones
30PRINTERSNetwork printers
40GUESTGuest devices and the guest wireless network
99MGMTSwitch and AP management. Untagged (native) on every trunk
999PARKINGUnused ports. Nothing is routed to it

For a trunk, the VLAN column is its native VLAN. For an access port, it is the access VLAN.

PortConnected deviceModeAccess / native VLANVoice VLAN
Gi0/1Uplink to the core switch, carries every VLAN
Fa0/1IP phone, with a staff PC plugged into the phone's PC port
Fa0/2Staff PC
Fa0/3Network printer
Fa0/4Access point broadcasting the staff and guest SSIDs, each mapped to its own VLAN. The AP's management address is untagged
Fa0/5Guest kiosk PC in the lobby
Fa0/6Nothing connected
Explanation
  • Trunks carry several VLANs to another VLAN-aware device: the core switch, and the AP, which puts each SSID on its own VLAN. The native VLAN is the one sent untagged, so it is 99 on both, where the requirement put management traffic.
  • The phone port stays an access port. The phone tags its own traffic with the voice VLAN (20) and passes the PC's traffic through untagged on the access VLAN (10). On a Cisco switch that is switchport access vlan 10 plus switchport voice vlan 20.
  • Every single-purpose device gets an access port in its own VLAN: staff PC in 10, printer in 30, kiosk in 40.
  • The unused port goes into the parking VLAN rather than VLAN 1, so anyone who plugs in lands on a VLAN with no services. In production you would also shutdown it.
  • Why not VLAN 1 anywhere: it is the default for every port, so leaving devices or the native VLAN on it makes VLAN hopping and accidental access easier.
PBQ 2 · Simulation

Building 2 printers disappeared

After last night's change window, nobody can print to the printers in Building 2, which are on VLAN 30. Staff PCs in Building 2 (VLAN 10) still work, and printers in Building 1 work. SW-DIST in Building 1 connects to SW-BLDG2 over a trunk.

Find the problem and choose the command that fixes it without breaking anything else.

Console
Explanation
  • Compare both ends: SW-DIST allows 10, 30, and 99 on its trunk. SW-BLDG2 allows only 10 and 99, so VLAN 30 frames are dropped at SW-BLDG2's end. VLAN 30 exists on SW-BLDG2, the printer ports are in it, and the printers' MACs are learned there, so the access side is fine. Both native VLANs are 99.
  • The trap: switchport trunk allowed vlan 30 replaces the allowed list with only VLAN 30. Printing comes back, and staff PCs and switch management in Building 2 go down. add appends to the existing list.
  • Verify with show interfaces trunk, which lists the allowed VLANs per trunk. show vlan brief lists access ports only and does not show trunks at all, so it cannot confirm this fix.
