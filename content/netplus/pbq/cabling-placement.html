PBQ 1 · Cabling

Cable the new campus

You are specifying the cabling for a new campus. Choose the most appropriate cable for each link, then the connector for each description. Use the cheapest option that meets the requirement. Not every item is used.

Explanation
  • 1.8 km: beyond copper and multimode at 10 Gbps. Single-mode fiber covers kilometres.
  • 250 m with SR optics: SR transceivers are multimode. OM4 carries 10 Gbps up to about 400 m and is cheaper to light than single-mode.
  • 2 m in the rack: a direct attach copper (twinax) cable is the cheapest high-speed link at that distance. It has transceivers built into each end.
  • Air-handling space: that is a plenum, and fire code requires plenum-rated (CMP) jacket, which gives off less toxic smoke. PVC is not allowed there.
  • Electric motors create EMI. Shielded twisted pair protects the signal. Cat 5e PVC is unshielded.
  • Cable modem: RG-6 is the standard for broadband and satellite. RG-59 is an older, thinner cable used for short analog video runs.
  • Connectors: LC is "little connector", SC is "square/stick and click", ST is "stick and twist". MPO holds many fibers, commonly 12 or 24, in one ferrule. RJ11 is the 6-position telephone connector and does not fit an Ethernet drop.

Explanation
  • Next-generation firewall at the edge: it separates the three zones, and "by application and user" is what makes it next-generation rather than a plain port filter.
  • VPN headend (concentrator) at the edge, where remote users' tunnels arrive from the internet.
  • Load balancer in front of the server farm. Health checks are how it takes a failed server out of rotation.
  • IDS, not IPS: it gets a copy of traffic from a SPAN port, so it is out of band and can only alert. An IPS sits inline so it can block.
  • Wireless LAN controller for lightweight APs, which depend on it for configuration.
  • Proxy server: it makes requests on users' behalf, which is where content filtering and caching happen.
  • Unused: a hub repeats every frame to every port and has no place in a modern design. The modem belongs on the ISP side of the router, not inside the network.
