REMEMBER THIS
Cat 6A = 10 GbE up to the full 100m run. Cat 6 = 10 GbE only to 55m. Cat 5e = 1 GbE max. If a question pairs "10 Gbps" with "100 meters", the answer is Cat 6A.

T568A & T568B Wiring Standards

TIA/EIA-568 defines two pinout patterns for RJ45 connectors. Either standard works — the choice must be consistent throughout the installation.

StandardPins 1–2Pins 3–6When to Use

T568AGreen/White, GreenOrange/White, OrangeRequired for U.S. government installations; common in some international standards.
Straight-Through vs Crossover Cables

Straight-through: both ends use the same wiring standard. Connects unlike devices (PC → switch, switch → router). Crossover: one end T568A, the other T568B — connects like devices (switch → switch, PC → PC). Modern switches with Auto-MDIX detect and compensate, so crossover cables are rarely needed today.

Coaxial Cable

A central copper conductor surrounded by insulation, a braided metal shield, and an outer jacket. The shield reduces interference. Used for cable TV, legacy Ethernet, and CCTV.

TypeImpedanceConnectorUse Case
RG-675 ΩF-type (threaded)Cable TV, satellite dish, cable modem. Standard residential/commercial coax today.
RG-5975 ΩF-type or BNCOlder analog CCTV/video. Lower shielding than RG-6 — largely replaced.
RG-5850 ΩBNC (bayonet)Legacy 10BASE2 (Thinnet) Ethernet. Rarely seen today.
F-type vs BNC

F-type: threaded screw-on connector used on RG-6 for cable TV and satellite. BNC: quarter-turn bayonet lock used on RG-58 for legacy Ethernet and test equipment. Different connector always signals a different use case.

PBQ Prep — Cable Identification

Expect a drag-and-drop PBQ where you match cable/connector types to scenarios:

  • Scenario: "Run 10GbE for 80 meters within a building" → Cat 6A (UTP or STP)
  • Scenario: "Connect two data centers 15km apart" → SMF fiber with LC connectors
  • Scenario: "Cable through HVAC plenum space" → Plenum-rated Cat 6A
  • Scenario: "SFP+ port on switch, 300m to another building" → MMF fiber, SFP+ transceiver, LC connectors
Exam Traps
  • Cat 6 is only 10 Gbps for 55 meters — not 100m. Cat 6A goes 10 Gbps for the full 100m.
  • Plenum cable is required in air-handling spaces — not just "expensive cable." Fire codes mandate it.
  • SMF = yellow jacket (usually). MMF = orange (OM2/OM3) or aqua (OM4). But cable color can vary — know the specs.
  • RJ45 ≠ RJ11. RJ45 = 8 pins (Ethernet). RJ11 = 6 pins (telephone).
  • LC connector is small (used by SFP ports). SC is square/larger. ST is round with a bayonet twist.
  • T568B is the commercial standard. A crossover cable = T568A on one end, T568B on the other. Straight-through uses the same standard on both ends.
  • BNC = coaxial connector for legacy Ethernet (RG-58 / 10BASE2). F-type = coaxial for cable TV/satellite (RG-6). Different connectors, different use cases.
  • DAC (Direct Attach Copper) = twinaxial cable with SFP+ ends, max ~7 meters. Much cheaper than fiber for short data center runs.
  • 802.11ac (Wi-Fi 5) is 5 GHz only. 802.11n (Wi-Fi 4) was the first dual-band standard (2.4 GHz and 5 GHz).

Exam Traps — Wireless WAN
  • GEO satellite ≈ 600 ms RTT — disqualifies VoIP (max ~150 ms one-way). LEO (Starlink) is low enough for VoIP.
  • Cellular (4G/5G) is a valid SD-WAN transport option — commonly used as backup or primary WAN link.
  • mmWave = very high throughput but very short range — not suitable for wide-area coverage.

  • Cat 5e = 1G/100m | Cat 6 = 10G/55m | Cat 6A = 10G/100m | Cat 8 = 40G/30m
  • SMF = laser, long distance, single path | MMF = LED, short distance, multi-path
  • LC = small, data center favorite | SC = square | ST = twist lock (bayonet)
  • Plenum = air-handling spaces | Riser = between floors | PVC = conduit only
  • SFP = 1G | SFP+ = 10G | QSFP = 40G | QSFP28 = 100G
  • GEO satellite ≈ 600 ms RTT — disqualifies VoIP | LEO (Starlink) ≈ 20–40 ms — usable
  • 4G/5G cellular = valid SD-WAN transport option
  • T568B = commercial standard | T568A = government | Crossover = T568A↔T568B | Auto-MDIX eliminates need for crossover cables
  • RG-6 + F-type = cable TV/satellite | RG-58 + BNC = legacy 10BASE2 Ethernet
  • 802.3: 100BASE-TX = 100M Cat5 | 1000BASE-T = 1G Cat5e | 10GBASE-T = 10G Cat6A | 10GBASE-SR = 10G MMF (300m)
  • DAC = twinaxial + SFP+ ends, ≤7m, no module needed | FC transceiver = SAN storage (not Ethernet)
  • 802.11n = Wi-Fi 4, first dual-band | 802.11ac = Wi-Fi 5, 5 GHz only, MU-MIMO | 802.11ax = Wi-Fi 6, OFDMA | 802.11be = Wi-Fi 7, MLO
