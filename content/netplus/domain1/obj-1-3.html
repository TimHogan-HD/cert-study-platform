Objective 1.3

Network Topologies & Types

Topology describes how devices are physically and logically connected. Physical topology is how cables are run; logical topology is how data flows.

LAN Topologies

TopologyDescriptionAdvantagesDisadvantages
Star All devices connect to a central switch/hub Easy troubleshoot; one device failure doesn't affect others Central switch = single point of failure
Bus All devices on one shared cable (terminated at both ends) Simple, cheap, minimal cabling Collision-prone; cable break kills entire network. Legacy (10BASE-2 coax).
Ring Devices connected in a circle; data travels in one direction Predictable performance; no collisions with token passing Single break disrupts entire ring (unless dual-ring / FDDI)
Mesh (Full) Every device connects to every other device Maximum redundancy; no single point of failure Expensive; # connections = n(n-1)/2; complex management
Mesh (Partial) Some but not all devices have redundant paths Balance of cost and redundancy Some paths remain single points of failure
Hybrid Combination of topologies (star-bus, star-ring) Flexible; most modern networks are hybrid Complex design and troubleshooting

WAN Topology Types

WAN TypeDescriptionUse Case
Point-to-PointDedicated link between two sitesBranch to HQ connection, T1/E1 leased lines, fiber
Hub-and-SpokeCentral hub site connects to multiple spoke sitesCost-effective for many branches; spokes can't communicate without going through hub

KEY DISTINCTION
What breaks when one device fails? Bus: a single break splits the network in two. Ring: one failed node breaks the whole loop (unless dual-ring). Star: only the failed node loses connectivity — the rest keep working. This is why star is the dominant LAN topology.
⚠ Exam Traps
  • Star topology = most common physical LAN topology today. The "hub" in the center of a star is now a switch.
  • Bus topology is obsolete but still tested. Know it uses terminators at both cable ends.
  • Full mesh connections: a network of 6 devices needs 6×5/2 = 15 connections.
  • SOHO routers combine router + switch + WAP — they span multiple OSI layers.
Quick Recall — 1.3
  • Star = most common LAN; central device failure = total outage
  • Bus = legacy coax, terminators at ends; cable break = full outage
  • Full mesh connections = n(n-1)/2
  • WAN types: point-to-point, hub-and-spoke, full mesh, MPLS, SD-WAN
  • SAN = storage network (Fibre Channel / iSCSI), not a LAN
